About

I'm Matthew Collaro, a systems engineer working on embedded systems, systems architecture, and model-based engineering. My work sits at the seam where engineering programs usually break: between the SysML model and the specification, between the specification and the test, and between the test and the evidence that the test actually proved something.

The through-line is a conviction that traceability shouldn't be a documentation task. On most programs it's treated as one — engineers do the real work in their models and on their test benches, and then somebody reconstructs the links afterward, from memory, under deadline. That reconstruction is where the errors get in, and it's why a requirement can sit unsatisfied for two review cycles before anyone notices.

I'd rather the links be a side effect of the engineering itself — captured when the decision is made, checked continuously, and surfaced the moment they break. That's the problem I've spent my career on, across automotive, commercial aviation, and aerospace manufacturing, and it's what I'm building tooling for now.
